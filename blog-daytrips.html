Best Day Trips
from Mangalore

Complete Guide · 7 min read · Updated 2025
← Back to Blog
Mangalore's biggest advantage isn't just what's inside the city — it's everything within a 2-3 hour radius. Ancient temples, misty hill stations, coffee estates, pristine beaches and a 1000-pillar Jain temple are all within easy reach. If you have an extra day on your trip, here's exactly where to go.

Coastal Karnataka and the surrounding Western Ghats hold some of South India's most underrated destinations. Because Mangalore sits right where the Ghats meet the sea, you get an unusual mix of options — pilgrimage towns, hill treks, and beach getaways, often all within the same two-hour drive. Here are the six best day trips from the city, ranked by distance.

The Best Day Trips

1. Udupi
Temple Town
60 km from Mangalore
1.5 hrs drive
Half day needed

Udupi needs no introduction to anyone who loves South Indian food — this is the birthplace of "Udupi cuisine," the style behind most South Indian restaurants worldwide. But beyond the food, the Krishna Matha temple here is genuinely beautiful, with its gold-plated chariot, the famous Kanakana Kindi (Kanakadasa window), and a centuries-old tradition of feeding thousands of visitors daily for free.

Combine your temple visit with a stop at Malpe Beach nearby, and if you have extra time, take a short boat ride to St. Mary's Island to see its unique hexagonal rock formations — a geological rarity found in only a few places on Earth.

Don't miss: Krishna Matha Temple, Malpe Beach, St. Mary's Island, authentic Udupi cuisine
2. Dharmasthala
Pilgrimage
75 km from Mangalore
2 hrs drive
Half to full day

One of India's most visited pilgrimage towns, Dharmasthala is home to the Manjunatha Temple — unique for being managed by a Jain family while devotees of all faiths are welcomed and fed at the temple's massive free dining hall, which serves thousands daily. The town carries a deeply peaceful, communal energy that's worth experiencing even if you're not particularly religious.

Nearby, don't miss the 39-feet tall monolithic statue of Bahubali at Karkala-adjacent Venur, and the scenic drive through the Western Ghats foothills on the way there.

Don't miss: Manjunatha Temple, free community meal (annadana), Manjusha Museum
3. Karkala
Heritage & Statues
50 km from Mangalore
1 hr drive
Half day

Karkala is home to one of the tallest monolithic statues of Gomateshwara (Bahubali) in India, standing 42 feet tall atop a hill called Gomatagiri — the view from up there over the surrounding green hills is stunning. The town is also home to the Chaturmukha Basadi, a unique four-faced Jain temple, and the centuries-old Attur Church, an unusual coastal Gothic structure.

Karkala is small, quiet, and rarely crowded — making it perfect for a relaxed half-day trip combined with Moodbidri on the way.

Don't miss: Gomateshwara statue, Chaturmukha Basadi, Attur Church
4. Moodbidri
Jain Heritage
35 km from Mangalore
45 min drive
2-3 hours

The closest day trip on this list, Moodbidri is famous for the Saavira Kambada Basadi — the "Thousand Pillar Temple" — a stunning Jain temple complex where every single pillar is carved differently, no two alike. It's an architectural marvel that often gets overlooked compared to bigger temples, but is genuinely one of the most impressive heritage sites near Mangalore.

Because it's so close, Moodbidri makes a great add-on to a half-day trip — pair it with Karkala for a fuller day out.

Don't miss: Saavira Kambada Basadi (1000 Pillar Temple), the carved bronze Manasthambha pillar
5. Kudremukh
Trekking & Nature
95 km from Mangalore
2.5 hrs drive
Full day

For nature lovers and trekkers, Kudremukh is the best day trip from Mangalore by far. Named for its horse-face shaped peak ("Kudure" means horse in Kannada), this region in the Western Ghats is part of a UNESCO-recognised biodiversity hotspot — rolling grassland hills, dense shola forests, and some of the cleanest air you'll breathe in Karnataka.

The trek to the peak requires forest permits and is best done early morning — start by 6 AM. Even if you skip the trek, the drive through Kudremukh National Park, with mist rolling over the hills, is reason enough to come.

Don't miss: Kudremukh Peak trek, Western Ghats viewpoints, Bhagavati Falls nearby
6. Coorg (Kodagu)
Hill Station — Overnight Recommended
135 km from Mangalore
3.5 hrs drive
1-2 days

Technically too far for a comfortable single-day round trip, Coorg deserves a mention because it's the most popular extended trip people take from Mangalore. Rolling coffee estates, misty hills, waterfalls and a refreshingly cool climate make it one of South India's most loved hill stations. If you have an extra day or two on your itinerary, it's absolutely worth the detour.

If you do want to attempt it as a long day trip, start by 6 AM and limit yourself to Abbey Falls and a coffee estate visit near Madikeri rather than trying to see everything.

Don't miss: Abbey Falls, coffee plantations, Raja's Seat, Madikeri Fort
Planning Tip

If you're short on time, combine Karkala and Moodbidri into a single half-day trip — they're only 15 km apart and both can be covered in under 4 hours including travel. For a full day out, Udupi and Dharmasthala can also be combined since they're connected by the same highway, though it makes for a long day.

How to Get There

All these destinations are well connected by road from Mangalore. You can either rent a self-drive car, book a cab for the day (most local operators charge a flat day-rate that includes waiting time), or take a KSRTC bus to the bigger towns like Udupi and Dharmasthala. For Kudremukh and Coorg, a private cab or your own vehicle is strongly recommended since public transport options are limited in these hilly stretches.

Quick Checklist Before You Go

  • Start early (by 7-8 AM) for any day trip beyond 50 km
  • Carry cash — smaller towns have limited card/UPI acceptance
  • Dress modestly for temple visits (Udupi, Dharmasthala, Karkala, Moodbidri)
  • Carry water and snacks for Kudremukh — very few shops en route
  • Check forest permit requirements before attempting the Kudremukh trek
  • Fill your tank before leaving the city — fuel stations are sparse in the Ghats

Mangalore's location is genuinely one of its best-kept secrets — you're never more than a couple of hours away from a temple a thousand years old, a peak in the Western Ghats, or a coffee estate in the clouds. Pick one of these based on how much time you have left, and you'll come away with a much fuller picture of coastal Karnataka.

Your Perfect 3 Days
in Mangalore

Day-by-Day Plan · 6 min read · Updated 2025
← Back to Blog
Three days is exactly the right amount of time for a first visit to Mangalore — enough to see the beaches, the temples, the culture, and eat your way through the city without rushing. This itinerary is built the way locals would plan it: slow mornings, full afternoons, and evenings that linger by the sea.

Mangalore is a city that rewards a slow pace. It isn't trying to impress you with monuments or theme parks — it impresses you with a quiet confidence. The food is extraordinary, the coastline is beautiful, the culture is ancient and alive, and the people are genuinely warm. Give it three days and it will give you memories that last much longer.

01
Day One
Beaches, Sunsets & Fresh Seafood
Arrive, settle in, and let the Arabian Sea do the rest
Morning — 8:00 AM
Breakfast: Mangalore Buns + Filter Coffee
Start the way every Mangalorean does — with soft, sweet Mangalore Buns (banana puris) and a strong filter coffee at a local darshini. Woodlands Hotel or any neighbourhood tiffin centre will serve this for under Rs. 80. Don't skip the coffee — Mangalore's filter coffee culture is as serious as it gets.
Try: Mangalore Buns, Idli with sambar, Filter Coffee
Mid-Morning — 10:00 AM
Panambur Beach — Water Sports & Shoreline Walk
Head to Panambur Beach — Mangalore's most popular and well-equipped beach. The water sports (jet-skiing, banana boat, parasailing) are fully operational in peak season and genuinely fun. Even if you skip the sports, the walk along the long shoreline with the Western Ghats visible in the distance is worth the trip. Pick up roasted corn or tender coconut from the beach stalls.
Afternoon — 1:00 PM
Lunch: Fish Thali at a Local Restaurant
Head back towards the city for a proper Mangalorean fish thali lunch. This typically includes rice, dal, fish curry, a piece of fried fish, vegetable sides, and papad. It is a generous, completely satisfying meal. The restaurants near Hampankatta or Balmatta Road do excellent versions for Rs. 120 to 200.
Try: Fish thali, Bangude masala, Kane fry, Neer Dosa
Afternoon — 3:00 PM
Tannirbhavi Beach — The Ferry Crossing
Take an auto to the Panambur ferry point and board the short boat crossing over the Gurupura River to Tannirbhavi Beach. This ferry ride — with the river widening out to meet the sea, fishing boats in the distance, and the coastline stretching ahead — is one of the quiet highlights of any Mangalore trip. The beach itself is calmer, cleaner, and far less crowded than Panambur.
Evening — 5:00 PM
Sunset at Tannirbhavi
Stay at Tannirbhavi for the sunset. The western-facing position means you get an unobstructed view of the sun dropping into the Arabian Sea — golden light, gentle waves, barely any crowd. This is the moment people come back to Mangalore for. Take your time.
Night — 7:30 PM
Dinner: Gadbad Ice Cream at Pabbas
End Day 1 at Pabbas — the legendary ice cream parlour that invented the Gadbad. Order the original Gadbad and eat it slowly. The layers of ice cream, jelly, fruit and nuts are meant to be experienced, not rushed. A perfect end to a beach day.
Must order: Original Gadbad Ice Cream
Day 1 Tip

The Tannirbhavi ferry stops running after 6:30 PM — plan your sunset accordingly and make sure you board the return ferry before dark. The last ferry back is usually around 6:45 PM.

02
Day Two
Temples, Heritage & City Streets
Step into the ancient soul of Tulu Nadu
Morning — 7:30 AM
Kadri Manjunath Temple
Start early at Kadri Manjunath Temple — one of the oldest temples in coastal Karnataka, believed to be over 1,000 years old. The temple complex is beautiful in the morning light, with a peaceful tank, ancient bronze statues of Lokeshwara, and the forested Kadri Hills rising behind it. Mornings are quiet here, with just devotees and the sound of bells. Dress modestly and remove footwear at the entrance.
Mid-Morning — 10:00 AM
Kudroli Gokarnath Temple
Drive to Kudroli Gokarnath Temple in the city centre — a stunning Dravidian-style temple that is famous for its elaborately decorated gopuram (tower) and especially spectacular during Navratri and Dasara when the entire complex is lit up and the Pilivesha (Tiger Dance) performers take to the streets. Even outside festival season, the temple's architecture and the energy of the surrounding market area are worth exploring.
Afternoon — 12:30 PM
Lunch: Kori Rotti at Machali
This is non-negotiable. Walk to Machali restaurant and order Kori Rotti — the dry rice wafers soaked in the rich chicken curry of Tulu Nadu. If you eat only one full meal in Mangalore, let this be it. The restaurant is no-frills, always busy, and consistently excellent. Arrive slightly before 1 PM to avoid the peak lunch rush.
Order: Kori Rotti, Kane Fry, Chicken Sukka
Afternoon — 2:30 PM
Car Street — Old Mangalore Exploration
Walk through Car Street, the historic commercial heart of old Mangalore. This narrow, vibrant street is lined with shops selling everything from gold jewellery to fresh produce, with the smell of incense, frying snacks, and sea air all mixed together. Stop at a street stall for Charmuri — the spiced puffed rice snack that is Mangalore's answer to bhelpuri. Then explore the old Rosario Cathedral and the surrounding heritage lanes.
Try: Charmuri, fresh coconut water, roasted peanuts
Late Afternoon — 4:30 PM
Polali Rajarajeshwari Temple (Optional Day Trip)
If you have energy and want to see something truly off the tourist track, take an auto or cab to Polali — about 25 km from the city centre. The Rajarajeshwari Temple here sits on the banks of the Phalguni River surrounded by thick greenery, and the boat ride to reach the temple is an experience entirely unique to this region. Peaceful, spiritual, and completely beautiful.
Evening — 7:00 PM
Dinner: Neer Dosa Thali
A lighter dinner tonight — find a good local restaurant serving a Neer Dosa thali with coconut chutney, fish curry, and a vegetable side. The contrast with Kori Rotti at lunch is intentional — Neer Dosa is delicate and light, the perfect evening meal after a day of temple-hopping.
Day 2 Tip

Kadri Temple is most atmospheric very early in the morning (between 7 and 8 AM) when morning prayers are ongoing and the temple tank reflects the light beautifully. If you can manage an early start, it's completely worth it.

03
Day Three
Culture, Surathkal & Sweet Goodbyes
Your last full day — make it count
Morning — 9:00 AM
Breakfast: Idli, Vada, Filter Coffee
A classic South Indian breakfast at a neighbourhood darshini — soft idlis, crispy vadas, fresh sambar and chutneys. Simple, cheap, and deeply satisfying. This is what mornings smell like in Mangalore.
Mid-Morning — 10:30 AM
Surathkal Beach — Lighthouse & Rocky Shore
Head 15 km north of the city to Surathkal Beach — a completely different kind of coastal experience from Panambur. Here, dramatic rocky outcrops line the shore, the historic Surathkal Lighthouse stands tall against the sky, and the beach itself is quiet, wild, and gorgeous. Walk along the rocks carefully at low tide for some of the best coastal photography in Karnataka.
Afternoon — 1:00 PM
Lunch: Chicken Sukka + Rice at a Local Hotel
Try Chicken Sukka today — the dry coconut-spiced preparation that is very different from anything you'd get in north Karnataka or north India. Pair it with white rice, a simple dal, and a piece of fried fish on the side. A meal that quietly makes you realise Mangalorean food is in a league of its own.
Afternoon — 3:00 PM
Someshwara Beach + Temple
Drive south to Someshwara Beach — 12 km from the city — to see one of the most atmospheric spots in all of Coastal Karnataka. Large smooth black rocks scattered across the shoreline, powerful waves crashing around them, and the ancient Somanatha Temple sitting dramatically right at the water's edge. The combination of the temple bells, the sea spray, and the smell of incense is unlike anywhere else in the region. Spend time here.
Evening — 5:30 PM
Shopping: Take Mangalore Home
Head back to the city for some last-minute shopping. Mangalore is famous for its cashews (some of the best in India come from here), Byadagi chilli powder and spice mixes, locally made pickles (especially fish pickle), kachampuli (the sour tamarind extract used in coastal curries), and traditional Tulu Nadu textiles. The shops near Car Street and Hampankatta have all of this.
Night — 7:30 PM
Final Dinner: Go Back to Your Favourite
On your last evening, go back to wherever you had the meal that struck you most. That's the best thing about a 3-day trip — you already know what you love. Then end it with a scoop (or two) of Tender Coconut ice cream at Naturals, sitting outside and watching Mangalore go about its evening. That's the trip. That's the city.
End with: Tender Coconut ice cream at Naturals, Balmatta Road
Day 3 Tip

Buy your Mangalore cashews from a trusted shop near Car Street rather than the airport — you'll get better quality for less than half the price. Ask the shopkeeper to vacuum-pack them for travel. They last weeks and make for the best gift you can bring back from Mangalore.

Before You Go — Practical Tips

  • Stay near Hampankatta or Kadri — central location, easy to get everywhere
  • Use Ola or auto-rickshaw for getting around — both are widely available
  • Carry cash — most local restaurants and beach stalls don't accept UPI
  • Best season: October to February for beaches and outdoor activities
  • Temples require modest clothing — carry a dupatta or light scarf
  • Most restaurants close between 3 PM and 6 PM — plan meals accordingly
  • The ferry to Tannirbhavi stops by 6:45 PM — don't miss the last return
  • Book your return train or bus early — Mangalore trains fill up fast in peak season

Mangalore doesn't announce itself. It doesn't have a single iconic image or a headline attraction. What it has is something better — a complete, deeply satisfying experience of coastal Karnataka that leaves you feeling like you discovered something real. Three days here is just enough to fall in love with it. Not enough to want to leave.

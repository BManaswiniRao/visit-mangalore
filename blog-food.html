10 Must-Try Foods in
Mangalore

Complete Food Guide · 7 min read · Updated 2025
← Back to Blog
Ask any food lover who has been to Mangalore and they will tell you the same thing — they came for the beaches and stayed for the food. Mangalorean cuisine is one of India's most underrated and deeply flavourful regional traditions, built on fresh coastal seafood, fragrant coconut, hand-ground spices, and generations of kitchen wisdom passed down in Tulu Nadu homes.

Mangalore sits at the intersection of the sea and the spice trade — and its food reflects exactly that. The cuisine here is bold but balanced, fiery but never overwhelming, and rooted in ingredients that have been sourced locally for centuries. If you eat only one thing in Mangalore, you'll wish you had tried ten. So here are the ten you absolutely cannot leave without eating.

The 10 Foods You Must Try

1. Gadbad Ice Cream
Invented in Mangalore

The most famous thing to come out of Mangalore's food scene — the Gadbad is a towering dessert of layered ice cream, jelly, fresh fruit, nuts, dry fruits, and rose syrup, all piled into a tall glass. It was invented at Pabbas in 1978 and has been copied everywhere since, but nothing beats the original. The name "Gadbad" literally means "messy" in Tulu — and that's exactly what it is. Gloriously, chaotically delicious.

Where to eat: Pabbas Ice Cream, Hampankatta
02
2. Neer Dosa
Tulu Nadu Staple

Neer Dosa (literally "water dosa") is a thin, white, lacy crepe made from a simple batter of raw rice and water — no fermentation, no fuss. The result is soft, delicate, almost translucent, and incredibly light. It is served in stacks of three or four and eaten with fresh coconut chutney, fish curry, or chicken curry. Simple, humble, and deeply satisfying — this is everyday Mangalorean home food at its finest.

Where to eat: Most local hotels and tiffin centres across the city
3. Kori Rotti
Tulu Nadu's Signature Dish

If there is one dish that defines Tulu Nadu cuisine, it is Kori Rotti. Dry, crispy wafers made from boiled rice are broken and soaked in a rich, spiced chicken curry — the wafers soak up the gravy, turning from crisp to soft while still holding their shape. The curry itself is made with coconut milk, freshly ground spices, and slow-cooked chicken. It is an extraordinary combination of textures and flavours that you simply cannot find outside this region.

Where to eat: Machali Restaurant, Diana Restaurant, Hotel Srinivas
04
4. Mangalore Fish Curry
Coastal Classic

Made with fresh catch-of-the-day fish simmered in a tangy, fiery red gravy of ground coconut, dried red chillies, tamarind, and spices — Mangalore fish curry is the dish that locals eat every single day and never tire of. The use of kachampuli (Garcinia gummi-gutta, the local tamarind) gives it a sourness that no other curry in India has. Eat it with rice or Neer Dosa. You will want seconds.

Where to eat: Machali, Hotel Narayana, any local "meals" restaurant
05
5. Kane (Ladyfish) Tawa Fry
Mangalore's Favourite Fry

Kane fish — known as Ladyfish in English — is Mangalore's most beloved local fish. Small, slender, and silvery, Kane is marinated in a fierce red spice paste of Byadagi chillies, garlic, ginger, and turmeric, then shallow-fried on a tawa until the exterior is beautifully crispy and the inside stays moist and flaky. The crunch when you bite into it is unforgettable. This is the fish Mangaloreans are fiercely nostalgic about when they move away.

Where to eat: Machali, Hotel Woodlands, Gajalee
06
6. Chicken Sukka
Dry Coastal Curry

Unlike most Indian chicken dishes that are gravy-based, Chicken Sukka is a dry preparation — chicken cooked until most of the moisture evaporates, leaving pieces coated in a thick crust of toasted coconut and spices. The texture is chewy on the outside, tender inside, with a deep, smoky flavour from the freshly grated coconut that's been roasted right in the pan. Eat it as a side with rice and dal. Addictive is an understatement.

Where to eat: Hotel Taj Mahal, Janatha Deluxe, most non-veg restaurants
07
7. Mangalore Buns
Sweet Breakfast Staple

Don't let the name fool you — Mangalore Buns are not bread. They are soft, slightly sweet, fluffy deep-fried puris made from a dough of ripe mashed banana, flour, sugar, and a touch of cumin. The banana gives them a subtle sweetness and a gorgeous golden colour. They are served for breakfast with coconut chutney and are best eaten piping hot. One of those deceptively simple things that you'll find yourself thinking about long after you've left Mangalore.

Where to eat: Woodlands Hotel, Sri Krishna Bhat, most morning tiffin centres
08
8. Bangude Masala (Mackerel Curry)
Bold & Tangy

Bangude (Indian mackerel) is one of the most affordable and widely available fish on the Mangalore coast — and in the hands of a good Mangalorean cook, it becomes extraordinary. Bangude Masala is a thick, bold curry using the same red Byadagi chilli base as fish fry, but cooked down into a rich, sour, intensely flavoured gravy. The strong flavour of mackerel works perfectly against the tartness of kachampuli. Served with rice, it is a complete, honest coastal meal.

Where to eat: Any local restaurant serving a fish thali
9. Charmuri
Mangalore Street Food

Charmuri is Mangalore's answer to bhelpuri — and it is better. Puffed rice tossed with finely chopped onion, tomato, cucumber, raw mango, fresh coconut, coriander, lemon juice, a generous dose of chilli, and a handful of namkeen. It is assembled to order at street carts, given a vigorous shake in a steel vessel, and served in a paper cone. Cold, crunchy, spicy, tangy, and absolutely impossible to eat just one serving of. Head to Car Street for the best version in the city.

Where to eat: Car Street, Panambur Beach stalls, Hampi Nagar market
10. Tender Coconut Ice Cream
At Naturals, Mangalore

End your Mangalore food journey the way every local does — with a scoop of Tender Coconut ice cream at Naturals. Made purely from real tender coconut flesh and water, with no artificial flavours, it tastes exactly like biting into a fresh coconut on a hot day. Naturals also does a brilliant Sitaphal (custard apple) and raw mango flavour. Simple, pure, and genuinely the best ice cream in the city — right up there with Pabbas for your loyalty.

Where to eat: Naturals Ice Cream, Balmatta Road
Food Lover's Tip

Mangalore's best food is not in restaurants — it's in the small "darshinis" and "meals hotels" that locals eat at every day. Ask any auto driver where they eat lunch and follow them. You'll pay under Rs. 100 for a full fish thali that would cost ten times that elsewhere. The more modest the place looks, the better the food usually is.

The 4 Places You Must Visit

If you have limited time, these four spots cover the full spectrum of Mangalore's food culture:

Pabbas

The original Gadbad. Go for dessert — it is an institution.

Machali

Best place for Kori Rotti, fish fry and full coastal meals.

Car Street

Street food paradise — Charmuri, bhajis, fresh juice stalls.

Naturals

End every evening with a real-fruit ice cream. No exceptions.

Quick Checklist — Don't Leave Without Eating

  • Gadbad Ice Cream at Pabbas
  • Neer Dosa with coconut chutney and fish curry
  • Kori Rotti at a proper Tulu restaurant
  • Kane (Ladyfish) Tawa Fry
  • Charmuri from a Car Street stall
  • Mangalore Buns for breakfast
  • Tender Coconut ice cream at Naturals
  • A full fish thali at a local meals hotel

Mangalorean food rewards the curious — the more you explore, the more you find. Every neighbourhood has its own lunch spot, every family has its own fish curry recipe, and every street corner has something worth stopping for. Come hungry, eat everything, and don't plan a diet for at least three days after.
